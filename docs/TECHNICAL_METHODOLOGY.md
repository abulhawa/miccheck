# Audio analysis methodology

The production flow uses `analyzeGuidedSamples` in `packages/audio-metrics/src/guided.ts`. The legacy energy detector remains for baseline tests and package compatibility; it does not decide speech in the web recording flow.

## Capture and speech detection

The guided capture has two explicit stages: a three-second room check, then up to twenty seconds of voice recording started by the user. The room check is validated for speech before offering the voice step. Capture stops during the preparation pause; the same microphone stream stays connected until completion, cancellation, or an error. The two captured intervals are concatenated for analysis and playback, with the measured room interval duration passed to the worker. No preparation audio is included. AudioWorklet provides raw mono PCM. The app requests echo cancellation, noise suppression, and automatic gain control to be disabled and records the actual track settings. If raw capture is unavailable, decoded MediaRecorder audio is a lower-certainty fallback.

A windowed-sinc low-pass resampler converts a copy to 16 kHz for Silero VAD v5 in a Web Worker. Inference uses 512-sample frames, 64 samples of context, and recurrent state. Speech starts at a 0.5 threshold; segments must span at least 160 ms, with short gaps merged. Once that minimum span is established, a 0.35 continuation threshold retains weaker speech frames. Confirmation resets when the segment ends; ambiguous evidence alone or an isolated high spike followed by weak evidence cannot start an accepted segment. No fixed boundary padding is added. These provisional rules improve the tested annotated interiors, but low-score onsets and noisy speech can still be missed; see [continuation evidence and limits](VAD_CONTINUATION_BENCHMARK.md). The original sample-rate PCM is used for measurements.

A grade requires at least one second of detected speech after calibration, at least one second of quiet calibration, and less than 150 ms of detected speech in that interval. Missing speech or contaminated calibration returns an explicit insufficient-evidence state. A model failure returns an error rather than substituting an invented grade.

## Measurements

- **Speech level:** RMS of samples in detected speech intervals after calibration, expressed in dBFS. It measures digital signal level, not physical sound pressure.
- **Noise floor:** RMS of the initial quiet interval. Later nonspeech runs are checked in 250 ms windows after excluding 300 ms around speech boundaries. At least 500 ms of usable quiet audio is required; two windows differing from calibration by more than 6 dB flag unstable noise and withhold the grade. A supplemental decrease check requires at least 500 ms (two quantized 250 ms windows) of consecutive disjoint 25 ms hops more than 6 dB below calibration, preventing fixed-window edge dilution from hiding sustained decreases. It retains the 300 ms guard and resets at every nonqualifying hop and quiet-run boundary; see [decrease validation](NOISE_DECREASE_BENCHMARK.md). A separate brief-event check uses 50 ms windows with a 25 ms hop, a 500 ms boundary guard, and nominally 100 ms of consecutive elevated-window coverage (>6 dB above calibration). The coverage requirement is one floored 50 ms window plus two nearest-sample 25 ms hops (three consecutive elevated windows), so rounding cannot add a fourth-window requirement at 22.05 kHz. Coverage describes overlapping analysis windows, not measured event duration. This catches tested brief increases without accumulating isolated spikes across pauses. Changes entirely below -60 dBFS are suppressed. Increases reaching above -60 dBFS compare against actual calibration RMS; decreases retain floored comparisons. Missing later quiet audio is explicitly unassessed, lowers diagnostic certainty, and prevents numerical take comparison without forcing a retry. These thresholds are provisional; events within guards, changes during speech, and speech detection errors can still escape the check.
- **SNR:** subtract quiet power from speech-interval power, then compare the remaining estimated signal power with quiet power. `signalRms = sqrt(max(0, speechRms² - quietRms²))`; SNR is `20 log10(signalRms / quietRms)`, bounded to -20 through 80 dB with numerical floors. Unusable quiet evidence withholds a grade.
- **Clipping:** fraction of detected speech samples with absolute amplitude at least 0.98, used for grading and advice. Separately, total near-full-scale sample duration across the entire recording (including calibration) is reported in seconds; duration is the number of qualifying samples divided by sample rate, excluding all below-threshold gaps. A clipping event is a maximal consecutive run of qualifying samples in the original recording PCM, regardless of sign. A single below-threshold sample ends an event; there is no minimum event length or gap merging. Event counts include calibration and nonspeech, and do not affect grading. The capture intervals are concatenated, so a run spanning their join is counted once. Adding nonspeech silence cannot dilute speech clipping. Threshold crossings indicate possible clipping, not proof of audible distortion; this heuristic can miss distortion below the threshold.
- **Hum:** the dominant Hann-windowed sinusoid near 50 or 60 Hz (±2 Hz, 0.5 Hz steps), normalized by coherent window gain and quiet-interval power, bounded to [0, 1]. This estimates narrowband mains-like energy, not its physical cause.
- **Echo:** the legacy autocorrelation estimator is experimental. Guided analysis measures original contiguous speech runs after calibration, then averages their scores weighted by sample count. Runs of 200 ms or less provide no full lag and are excluded; no eligible runs gives a zero score, not proof of absent echo. Pauses are never removed to create autocorrelation pairs. The estimator can confuse periodic voice structure with reflections. It is excluded from grading and actionable recommendations.

## Grade and certainty

The [controlled noise-stability benchmark](NOISE_STABILITY_BENCHMARK.md) documents the window and floor behavior, including the brief-event fix. The sensitive brief-event check runs only in pauses following detected speech, to avoid labelling soft initial speech missed by VAD as background noise. It requires a return to the calibration level before confirming a burst. An elevated short event cut off by capture end or a speech guard leaves stability unassessed unless other windows establish instability; this lowers certainty without an unsupported retry. Sustained checks also inspect the leading nonspeech interval. A stable assessment means no qualifying change detected in available quiet windows; shorter events, events before first speech, events inside guards, and low-level changes can be missed. Synthetic boundary annotations do not validate speech detection or justify representative accuracy claims.

Existing context-dependent deterministic thresholds turn speech level, clipping, and SNR into category ratings; the weakest applicable rating controls the grade. Thresholds live in `packages/audio-metrics/src/config.ts` and scoring modules. Recommendations favor free adjustments. The grade is not learned, professionally calibrated, or a universal microphone-quality measure.

Certainty describes available evidence, not whether the grade is good. It is at most medium for raw PCM with all three processing settings explicitly false; encoded, processed, or unknown settings produce low certainty. Representative speaker/room/device evaluation remains outstanding.

## Optional background classification

YAMNet runs on the quiet interval only when that interval is usable. The UI exposes a small subset (typing/keyboard, air conditioning, music) only when its mean model score exceeds 0.35 and exceeds speech/silence scores. Otherwise it returns unknown. These are tentative labels, not calibrated probabilities or validated source attribution. Classification failure does not discard successful speech analysis.

See `apps/web/public/models` for pinned model artifacts, hashes, and license notices, and [BENCHMARK.md](BENCHMARK.md) for the reproducible synthetic smoke evaluation.
